## Builder Lessons

1. Treat recent published examples as pattern evidence only. They can guide first-pass form, but they never prove the uploaded PDF's missing repository, box, folder, date, copy basis, classification, attachment treatment, or cross-reference.
2. Preserve source-family identity. Recent source notes distinguish Department of State lot files, Executive Secretariat files, Central Foreign Policy File records, Reagan Library NSC institutional files, Reagan Library staff/directorate files, Shultz/Hoover/private copies, Situation Room/watch-center records, Daily Diary/schedule evidence, DOD/CIA/agency records, Bush Presidential Records, H-Files, NSR Files, Scowcroft files, STARS records, public or printed sources, foreign-government records, allied-government records, UN/OAS/NATO/European Community/secretariat or other international-organization records, and source-image or visual-material lanes.
2a. Transition records, reader markings, and copy variants require source-family treatment. Recent volumes can print pre-inaugural or transition-era Vice Presidential/President-elect records, staff-file copies, routed/read-by copies, initials/signatures, stamped review notations, marginal comments, handwritten questions, and government-copy variants inside otherwise ordinary memoranda, letters, briefings, or attachment packets. Preserve transition status, source family, reader/recipient action, marginalia text and location, initials/signature basis, routing/read-by status, copy variant, attachment status, and target-volume placement separately; do not convert reader comments into body text, routing into approval, or Vice Presidential Records into ordinary Presidential Records.
2b. Volume source lists and finding aids are context, not selected documents. Recent volumes include `Sources` sections with public-source descriptions, repository families, lot files, Central Foreign Policy File reel explanations, unpublished-source lists, and published-source bibliographies. Preserve those as source_register_or_finding_aid evidence when uploaded as context; do not draft a manuscript annotation sheet or convert a listed repository/source family into a document-specific Source note without selected-document evidence.
2c. Declassification-process front matter is context, not a document-specific release certificate. Recent volumes include `About the Series`, editorial-methodology, and `Declassification Review` sections with RAC scan limitations, `Not found attached` caveats, bracket/excision conventions, review office, Executive Order basis, review date range, and volume-level counts for withheld or excised documents. Preserve those as declassification_packet evidence when uploaded as context; do not apply volume-level review claims to a selected document's release date, page count, declassification status, or attachment status without document-specific evidence.
2d. Incrementally published volumes require placeholder handling. Carter Volume XXVII, Western Europe, demonstrates that recent official EPUBs can contain cleared chapters alongside placeholder document pages stating that the document will be published once its chapter has been cleared. Preserve placeholder wording, chapter-cleared status, and partial Sources-section scope as `incremental_chapter_placeholder` / incremental_publication evidence; do not treat placeholder pages as OCR failures, withheld-in-full documents, or selected manuscript records.
2e. Legacy digitized volumes and microfiche preview editions require scan/status treatment. Quarterly-release evidence shows that history.state.gov can publish older printed volumes and microfiche supplements as newly digitized releases, including preview editions with front-matter booklets and PDFs of documents before full-text conversion. Preserve original print publication date, quarterly release or digitization date, preview-versus-full-text status, microfiche supplement status, front-matter booklet status, document-PDF bundle status, scan/OCR quality, page labels, and fiche/frame identifiers separately; do not use digitization date as the historical document date or recast a preview shell as a final annotation-sheet source.
2f. Full-volume and chapter exports require selection triage. Official ebook and status-page evidence shows that an operator can reasonably upload a full-content ebook/PDF, a full chapter export, a table of contents, or a document-list packet. Preserve volume title, chapter title, document list, page-numbering basis, full-volume/chapter status, front/back matter, ebook/export status, and publication/update date separately; do not draft one annotation sheet or use volume-level publication/update data as a selected document date, source path, declassification status, document number, or manuscript placement.
2g. history.state.gov web/print exports require web-artifact treatment. A browser-generated PDF from a history.state.gov document page can include useful selected-document text, but it also carries page titles, URL strings, print dates, headers/footers, navigation labels, and local export artifacts. Classify these uploads as `history_state_web_or_print_export`; draft only when the selected document body, heading, source note, and boundaries are visible or supplied. Otherwise use `web_print_export_triage` / `not_annotation_sheet_web_export_shell_only` and ask for the selected document page, static EPUB/TEI extract, OCR text, compiler source authority, or source image. Do not use URL/header/footer/print date as source provenance/document date.
2h. Fax sheets, routing slips, and cover sheets require wrapper treatment. Recent published source notes and compiler candidate packets show that wrapper pages can record delivery instructions, routing chains, presentation/read-by clues, page counts, S/S numbers, and attached-document references. Classify these uploads as `fax_or_transmission_cover_sheet` / `transmission_or_routing_cover_sheet`; use `cover_sheet_triage` / `not_annotation_sheet_cover_sheet_only` when no selected payload is present. Do not use fax/routing date, receipt stamp, delivery instruction, page count, or cover-sheet addressee as the selected document date, source provenance, approval, read status, or manuscript placement.
2i. Press releases, press guidance, and background briefings require public-release treatment. Recent Foundations-model volumes explicitly draw on press releases, press conferences and background briefings, and can print editorial-note excerpts from embargoed background briefings by senior officials. Classify these uploads as `press_release_or_background_briefing`; preserve release or briefing date/time, venue, speaker or attribution label, ground rules, embargo or release status, public transcript/version basis, carrier telegram or archival-copy lane, selected/excerpted/editorial-note status, and adjacent boundaries separately. Do not recast them as memcons, interviews, ordinary public speeches, public-affairs strategy memoranda, or archival source-note records.
3. Expect many PDF archetypes, not one document template: memoranda, telegrams, memcons, telcons, airgrams/despatches, non-papers/aide-memoires, Situation Room/watch-center products, shorthand meeting notes, memoranda for the record, record notes, night notes and evening reports, letters, presidential messages, diplomatic notes, minutes, recorded proceedings, tape transcripts, forum/seminar records, papers, prepared reports, research assessments, policy papers, draft/working-copy/version records, action memoranda, directive packages, policy-review/study directives, organization-management and administrative-process records, negotiating-instructions packages, congressional testimony and legal public sources, law-enforcement/counterterrorism case records, economic/financial/foreign-assistance budget records, human-rights/refugee/global-issues records, military-crisis and operational records, foreign-government and international-organization records, maps, photographs, charts, source images, talking points, public-affairs strategy and public-diplomacy/outreach plans, public speeches, remarks, statements, press releases, press guidance, background briefings, media items, President's Daily Brief and National Intelligence Daily articles or annexes, treaty texts, international agreements, treaty transmittals, personal diaries, memoirs, oral histories, retrospective accounts, editorial notes, appendix/facsimile records, handwritten notes, withheld-in-full selected-document placeholders, excerpted reports or omitted-body papers, and attachment packets.
4. Keep controlled annotation formulas controlled: `See Document [n].`, `See footnote [n], Document [n].`, `In telegram [n]...`, `Attached but not printed...`, `Not found.`, `Scheduled for publication in Foreign Relations...`, `Brackets are in the original.`, `Printed as Document [n].`, `No minutes were found.`, `Not found attached.`, and appendix cross-references require exact supporting evidence.
5. Distinguish page evidence from source-note prose. Release stamps, withdrawal sheets, local file paths, history.state.gov URLs, browser print headers/footers, print/export dates, HTML navigation labels, fax sheets, cover sheets, routing slips, receipt stamps, scan labels, FOIA markers, and export watermarks are evidence ledger material unless compiler/source authority says they belong in the final source note.
6. Preserve date bases separately. Recent volumes frequently separate document date, meeting date, transmission date, packet date, diary/schedule date, transmittal date, and publication or release date.
7. Daily Diary, schedule, calendar, and meeting-log records are support evidence unless selected directly. Recent volumes use them for date, time range, place, chronology, and sometimes attendance, while keeping them separate from substantive minutes, memoranda of conversation, later diary/memoir recollections, and negative-search claims such as `No substantive record of the conversation has been found.`
7a. Retrospective and personal accounts require account-specific extraction. Recent volumes can use personal diaries, memoirs, oral histories, later interviews, and edited-diary publications alongside official Daily Diary timing, no-minutes/no-memcon claims, handwritten-note evidence, and public statements. Preserve author, account type, entry/recollection date, publication date, page or folio locator, quote boundaries, and official-record gap separately; do not convert retrospective recollections into formal minutes, official transcripts, participant rosters, or approved decisions.
8. Directive and decision packages require directive-specific extraction. Recent volumes use NSDDs/NSDs, covering memoranda, annexes, tabs, distribution lists, paragraph classification markings, and printed-elsewhere relationships. Keep directive number, package layer, classification scope, distribution evidence, and cross-volume reference authority distinct.
8a. Policy reviews and study directives require tasking-specific extraction. Recent volumes can print National Security Reviews or National Security Study Directives with addressee lists, tasking bodies, terms of reference, responsible interdepartmental groups, due dates, required reports, printed tabs, attached-but-not-printed timetables, work plans, gap lists, prior-directive references, handling limits, and later decision-record or NSDD/NSD relationships. Preserve review tasking separately from final policy decisions and do not infer approval, resumption, tabled positions, negotiating instructions, or transmitted instructions from a requested review.
8b. Organization-management and administrative-process records require organization-specific treatment. Recent volumes can print NSC staff-structure memoranda, office-role/effective-date footnotes, review-board and management-reform records, recordkeeping caveats, committee/participant-list evidence, missing organizational tabs, and scheduled Organization and Management volume references. Preserve office/role, effective-date, staff-structure, committee, tab-status, recordkeeping, review-board/report, and related-volume target evidence separately; do not convert meeting-specific participation into standing membership, acting service into confirmed office, or scheduled publication/status language into printed claims.
9. Technical tables, charts, matrices, and list-heavy documents require layout preservation. Recent volumes include cost tables, column-level excisions, line/paragraph redactions, units, acronyms, and numeric comparisons where row/column relationships carry meaning. Preserve structure and flag OCR/layout uncertainty.
10. Intelligence and estimative records require analytic-source treatment. Recent volumes print DIA, CIA, INR, NIC, JCS, and other agency products with product numbers, information-as-of dates, paragraph markings, handling restrictions, not-declassified placeholders, receipt stamps, and agency-specific source paths. Keep those claims separate from ordinary classification and declassification fields.
10a. Draft, working-copy, and versioned records require version-status treatment. Recent volumes print selected drafts, uncoordinated intelligence estimates, redrafts, proposed texts, and draft-versus-final references. Preserve draft status, coordination status, covering memorandum evidence, final-version citation, version comparison, agency dissent or alternative view, omitted sections, and adjacent boundaries separately; do not silently treat a selected draft as final, coordinated, approved, sent, tabled, or published.
10b. Daily intelligence brief articles require article-versus-issue treatment. Recent volumes can print selected President's Daily Brief or National Intelligence Daily articles and annexes when the full daily publication is not filed with the collection or the article is found only in extracted form. Classify these uploads as `daily_intelligence_brief_or_pdb_article`; preserve the daily series, article or annex status, issue date, article title, source path, Top Secret/For the President Only/codeword or handling restrictions, not-declassified placeholders, and full-daily-version availability separately. Do not infer full PDB/NID contents, presidential receipt/read status, dissemination, or analytic coordination from the selected article alone.
11. Memcons, telcons, meeting minutes, and call records require meeting-specific extraction. Recent volumes use meeting time ranges, places, two-sided participant blocks, notetaker/interpreter labels, omitted-topic brackets, source-note meeting locations, drafter/clearance chains, full-record scheduled-publication notes, and negative-search notes such as `No minutes were found.` Keep those claims separate.
11a. Memoranda for the Record and record notes require recordkeeping-specific treatment. Recent volumes can print selected MFRs, notes for the record, records of discussion, expert-meeting records, and delegation record notes that document meetings or exchanges without using memcon/telcon or formal minutes form. Preserve record type, subject/title, meeting or session date range, drafter/preparer, attendee list, side labels, attachment status, negative no-memcon-search claims, and adjacent document boundaries separately; do not recast these records as formal minutes, memcons/telcons, telegrams, decision records, or briefing memoranda.
11b. Recorded proceedings and tape transcripts require transcript-source treatment. Recent volumes can print Open Forum, seminar, conference, or oral-proceeding records from tape transcripts with bracketed title-page or program omissions, off-the-record ground rules, moderator/chair framing, generic speaker labels, inaudible markers, laughter/applause cues, cover-letter circulation evidence, and decisions not to publish edited versions. Preserve those as transcript and publication-status evidence; do not treat an omitted title page as a bad upload, identify generic speakers without authority, convert off-the-record context into classification status, or merge a following numbered document into the selected proceeding.
12. Telegrams and cables require full header and routing extraction. Recent volumes include telegram number, origin/destination, transmission time, classification, precedence, CFPF/Electronic Telegrams identifiers, referenced telegrams, and page-boundary spillover. Keep transmission date, event date, referenced telegrams, and editorial ordering evidence distinct.
12a. Airgrams and despatches require non-telegram treatment. Recent volumes can print airgram numbers, subject/ref blocks, long-form summary/conclusion sections, source-note drafting/clearance/approval chains, typed signatures or `initialed for` claims, and reference-telegram footnotes without visible DTG, TAGS, precedence, channel, or complete telegram headers. Preserve the airgram number and subject/ref structure separately, and do not invent ordinary cable header metadata.
12aa. Prepared research reports and policy papers require report-specific extraction. Recent volumes print selected `Report Prepared in...`, `Paper Prepared in...`, office assessment, and staff-study records with report/control numbers, preparing offices, titles or subtitles, summaries, section headings, classification or portion markings, source paths, drafting/approval evidence, and reader marginalia. Preserve those fields separately; do not recast the report as a briefing memorandum, telegram, formal intelligence estimate, ordinary memorandum, or editorial note solely because the source office is analytic or the text is narrative.
12b. Non-papers and informal papers require informal-paper treatment. Recent volumes can print selected non-papers with no classification marking, no formal recipient or signature, handwritten presentation notes, Daily Diary timing support, and readout evidence that the paper was distributed or summarized in a meeting. Preserve the selected paper separately from the meeting record and do not invent correspondence or memorandum metadata.
12c. Situation Room and watch-center products require product-specific treatment. Recent volumes can print White House Situation Room notes/checklists, NMCC significant event reports, State Ops reports, and task-force situation reports that summarize telegrams, wire-service reports, intelligence feeds, notifications, or phone confirmations. Preserve watch-office product type, checklist/report title, as-of/event time, source path, classification, source telegram/report references, PSN or product identifiers, not-found product identifiers, marginalia, and adjacent boundaries separately, and do not recast the product as the telegram, wire report, or briefing memorandum it summarizes.
13. Treaty texts and international agreements require legal-instrument treatment. Recent volumes can print selected treaties, executive agreements, protocols, declarations, agreed statements, joint statements, memoranda of understanding, exchanges of notes, and signature pages with parties, signature place/date, article and paragraph hierarchy, definitions, numerical limits, verification or inspection provisions, authentic-language statements, signatory blocks, and source/publication basis. Preserve the legal text separately from transmittal letters, Senate messages, ratification records, public remarks, and article-by-article analyses.
13a. Treaty and transmittal packages require package-specific treatment. Recent volumes distinguish transmittal letters, treaty text, protocols, annexes, memoranda of understanding, article-by-article analyses, proposed presidential messages, documents integral to the treaty, documents associated with but not integral to the treaty, public transmittal, Senate ratification, and entry-into-force evidence. Keep selection status, attachment status, public-source evidence, archival source path, and follow-on legal status distinct; do not turn a transmittal letter into the treaty text, Senate message, ratification record, or entry-into-force notice.
14. Translated and foreign-language sources require translation-specific extraction. Recent volumes print translated minutes and diplomatic texts while preserving translation date, translating office, original-language or original-bracket status, omitted material, and attached-but-not-printed annexes. Keep original document date, meeting date, translation date, annex status, and page-boundary spillover distinct.
15. Public or printed sources can be selected documents in recent volumes. Do not force them into archival-source form when the selected record is a speech, remarks, address, statement, radio address, testimony, interview, treaty text, public paper, printed report, or editorial note. Preserve publication title, item title, event date, publication date, page number, issuing body, event time/venue, bracket-original or omitted-material notes, URL/granule metadata, archival-copy or Department-telegram transmission lanes, diary/schedule support, and newspaper follow-up citations separately.
15a. Public-affairs strategy and public-diplomacy/outreach plans require internal-strategy treatment. Recent volumes can select internal memoranda that analyze public attention, public support, message management, media strategy, outreach, or public-diplomacy planning because they reveal policy assumptions. Preserve author/recipient/subject, source path, classification, action/information routing, public-opinion evidence, audience/channel, proposed public line, and speech/statement/event relationship separately. Do not recast these plans as delivered public remarks, public-source items, final policy decisions, or ordinary talking points.
15b. Summit public statements and joint communiques require wrapper-versus-payload treatment. Recent volumes can print Department circular telegrams or public-source copies whose selected payload is a summit-issued joint statement, communique, leader statement, or release text for public or diplomatic briefing. Preserve the carrier telegram, public release basis, summit/event context, issuing leaders or parties, Begin/End text boundaries, public/private version, briefing-use instruction, and relationship to treaty or negotiation texts separately; do not collapse the item into an ordinary telegram, a signed treaty text, or a generic public-source citation.
15c. News conferences, media availabilities, and public Q&A require public-transcript treatment. Recent volumes can print a selected public transcript or an editorial note built around an excerpted exchange with reporters. Preserve event type, venue, event date/time, broadcast or live status, speaker, questioner identity, quoted question, quoted answer, public transcript citation, complete-text citation, excerpt scope, and editorial-note status separately. Do not recast the item as a memcon, interview, ordinary public speech, or archival source-note record.
15d. Press releases, press guidance, and background briefings require release-specific extraction. Recent volumes can rely on volume-context press releases, selected historical press releases, and background-briefing transcripts with embargo and senior-official attribution rules. Preserve issuing office, topic/title, release or briefing time, venue/briefing room, speaker or attribution label, public transcript or released-text basis, press pool/outlet, carrier telegram or archival-copy lane, and editorial-note status separately. Do not treat an Office of the Historian volume press release as a selected historical document unless the compiler selected it.
16. Appendix and facsimile records require special handling. Recent volumes use lettered appendix images, bracketed document numbers, sparse text layers around image-only pages, NLR/RAC/source-control identifiers, declassification stamps, and transcribed-copy cross-references. Keep the facsimile, transcription, image-page range, source-control evidence, and next-appendix boundary distinct; do not treat sparse OCR as a blank/truncated document or invent transcription text from difficult handwriting.
16a. Handwritten notes and editor-transcribed source records require transcription-specific treatment. Recent volumes can select handwritten notes or handwritten talking points while printing an editor transcription and pointing to an appendix image. Preserve selected-note identity, editor-transcription status, source-image or appendix relationship, uncertain or illegible readings, strikeouts, underlining, bullets, dashes, equals signs, indentation, source path, no-classification marking, meeting or diary support, and cross-references separately; do not recast the notes as a polished memorandum, public speech, formal meeting record, shorthand minutes, or source-image-only appendix.
17. Editorial notes can lack conventional `Source:` footnotes. Recent volumes use them for chronology, public-citation synthesis, scheduled-publication pointers, cross-volume references, and quoted public text. Do not force an editorial note into archival source-note form, and do not use a source-less editorial-note model for a memorandum, telegram, minutes, memcon, directive, or selected public text unless the target document is proved to be an editorial note.
18. Letters and presidential messages require correspondence-specific treatment. Recent volumes preserve salutations, closings, signatures, no-classification markings, backchannel or privacy-channel delivery notes, government-copy variants, unofficial translations, response cross-references, and scheduled-publication notes. Unitize initiating letters and replies separately, and keep letter date, delivery/transmission date, referenced-letter date, copy status, translation status, and adjacent document boundaries distinct.
19. Action memoranda, information memoranda, and decision-request memoranda require memo-wrapper and action-status treatment. Recent volumes preserve sender/preparer, office, recipient, subject line, action-versus-information status, principal recipient channel, issue/decision headings, drafter/clearance data, concurrence, stamped or initialed reader action, marginalia, source sensitivity markings, recommendation language, approval/disapproval/other lines, attached proposed outgoing memoranda or speech plans, referenced-document footnotes, nonaction notes, and adjacent-document boundaries. Keep information-only items distinct from action requests, and do not convert recommendations, proposed priorities, approval/disapproval lines, proposed attachments, or reader comments into completed decisions without visible approval or action-taken evidence.
19aa. Briefing memoranda and talking-points materials require briefing-specific treatment. Recent volumes preserve memo wrappers, recommended points, nested bullet/list hierarchy, Q&A or quotation blocks, review stamps, meeting/event context, and related-document cross-references. Keep preparation date, event/meeting date, referenced-document date, source-note evidence, and adjacent document boundaries distinct, and do not present proposed talking points as actions actually taken.
19a. Briefing-book and read-ahead preparation packets require packet-specific unitization. Recent volumes can print `Papers Prepared...` sets, Secretary/President/delegation/summit/trip briefing books, meeting books, or read-ahead packets with a cover/title list, multiple paper titles, selected printed papers, other papers attached but not printed, paper-specific footnotes, and separate drafting/clearance facts. Preserve cover list, paper inventory, printed paper status, unprinted listed-paper status, target recipient or event, packet date, individual paper date or undated status, and document boundaries separately; do not collapse the book into one ordinary memo or promote unprinted listed papers into selected text.
20. PROFS and staff electronic messages require electronic-message-specific treatment. Recent volumes preserve sender/recipient fields, copied recipients, subject lines, message system or printout evidence, source paths, thread/reply context, and classification or handling markings separately. Do not treat staff electronic messages as CFPF telegrams unless the visible source is a telegram/cable, and do not turn printout/export artifacts into source-note prose.
21. Printed attachments and enclosures require attachment-specific unitization. Recent volumes can print a letter, paper, tab, enclosure, or annex under the same document number while also noting other attached-but-not-printed materials. When the upload combines wrapper text, printed attachments, attached-but-not-printed papers, reader markings, and a neighboring record that refers back to the attachment, keep it in `attachment_packet` treatment until the compiler identifies the selected unit. Keep wrapper metadata, printed attachment metadata, attachment-specific classification, marginalia, omitted/not-declassified passages, and later `See Attachment, Document [n]` cross-references distinct.
22. Interviews, transcripts, and Q&A sessions require speaker-turn treatment. Recent volumes preserve interviewer/interviewee names, outlet or program, question labels, named speaker labels, no-classification markings, condensed or published versions, public-paper citations, omitted unrelated discussion, start/end times, and adjacent document boundaries. Do not recast these records as memcons or telcons unless the visible source identifies that form.
22a. Recorded proceedings and tape-transcript minutes require transcript-source treatment. Recent volumes can print Open Forum, seminar, briefing, or conference minutes from tape transcripts, with omitted title pages, moderator framing, off-the-record/confidentiality context, named and generic speaker labels, inaudible markers, laughter/applause cues, source-note forwarding/copy lanes, and explicit decisions not to publish edited versions. Preserve transcript-source and recording-quality facts separately; do not infer unidentified speaker names, complete participant rosters, public/edited versions, publication restrictions, or formal policy decisions from informal recorded discussion.
22b. Memoranda of telephone conversation require call-specific treatment. Recent volumes preserve exact call time ranges, side labels, endpoint locations, handoffs between speakers, notetaker/interpreter roles, source-note call-location claims, and exact end-time statements separately from ordinary meeting-place and attendance metadata. Do not treat a telephone call as an in-person meeting or infer shared physical attendance from the participant block.
23. Formal NSC, NSPG, PCC, Cabinet, interagency, board, and commission minutes require formal-minutes treatment. Recent volumes preserve institutional meeting body, subject or agenda blocks, role-based participant rosters, speaker-turn minutes, source-note meeting locations, separate Record of Decision references, decision/action/consultation language, and adjacent action-memo or directive boundaries. Do not turn role labels into personal names without authority, and do not treat tentative discussion as an approved decision unless the minutes or supplied decision record says so.
24. Records of Decision, Summaries of Conclusions, and action records require decision-record treatment. Recent volumes preserve decision formulas, issue headings, numbered agreed actions, responsible agencies, COB deadlines, no-decision/no-consensus language, attached-but-not-printed tab references, and adjacent same-form boundaries. Do not turn required reviews, pending agency comments, or deferred decisions into final policy approvals.
25. Covert-action authorization packets require Finding/MON-specific treatment. Recent volumes preserve proposed versus approved authority, prior Finding references, Memorandum of Notification options, Scope Papers, Reserve Release and funding evidence, Section 662 or congressional-notification language, no-final-signed-copy claims, attached-but-not-printed authorization instruments, operational not-declassified text, and printed attachment layers. Do not infer that a draft Finding or attached-but-not-printed MON was approved, signed, or reported to Congress without direct evidence.
26. Negotiating-instructions packages require delegation-guidance treatment. Recent volumes can print an NSDD or other wrapper that transmits overall instructions, draft Department telegrams, group-specific negotiating instructions, reftels, septels, attached-but-not-printed guidance, and proposed Begin/End text for negotiators. Keep wrapper authority, round/session, delegation addressee, draft-versus-sent telegram status, tabled proposal text, source path, and adjacent telegram boundaries distinct, and do not infer that authorized proposals were tabled, adopted, or transmitted without direct evidence.
27. Congressional testimony and legal public sources require congressional/legal treatment. Recent volumes can print testimony from Department of State Bulletin or hearing publications while also citing committee bodies, Congress/session, GPO page ranges, Public Papers messages to Congress, budget authority, authorization and appropriation stages, public-law or Statutes at Large citations, Presidential Determinations, and Federal Register publication status. Keep selected public text, legal status, bibliographic citation, and any archival-copy evidence separate, and do not infer approval, enactment, appropriation, advice-and-consent, notification, or publication from a request or policy discussion.
27a. Presidential Determinations and Federal Register notices need determination-specific treatment. A selected determination can include a number/date, statutory trigger, finding/waiver/certification language, report-to-Congress direction, signature block, attached statement of reasons or justification, source-note forwarding-memo context, and a directive that the determination shall be published in the Federal Register. Keep those fields separate and do not turn a publication directive into an actual Federal Register citation, publication date, congressional receipt, legal effectiveness, or fund-release claim unless the PDF or target-volume authority supplies that evidence.
28. Law-enforcement and counterterrorism case records require case-specific treatment. Recent volumes preserve hostage/hijacking case names, arrest warrants, Interpol red notices, provisional-arrest requests, extradition treaty limits, Hague/Montreal convention references, Title/U.S.C. charge citations, FBI/DEA/DOJ/OIA/S/CT lanes, counterterrorism office source families, not-declassified operational details, and scheduled terrorism-volume cross-references. Keep requests, legal status, liaison, and completed actions separate; do not infer arrests, extraditions, prosecutions, or convictions from a request or instruction.
29. Economic, financial, and foreign-assistance budget records require budget-specific treatment. Recent volumes preserve Treasury, AID, IDCA, OMB, State, Defense, and National Archives source lanes; ESF, FMS, PL-480, MAP, IMET, IMF, World Bank, IBRD, IDA, IDB, GCI, and SDR terms; fiscal-year cycles; dollar figures; attached-but-not-printed budget annexes; debt strategy and replenishment terms; and multi-bureau clearances. Keep proposals, requests, transmittals, authorizations, appropriations, obligations, disbursements, loan agreements, IFI decisions, and country allocations separate; do not infer later financial action from a budget request.
29aa. Human-rights, refugee, public-health, and global-issues records require issue-lane treatment. Recent volumes preserve Country Reports, AIDS/HIV and WHO/CDC terms, famine/disaster relief, PL 480/Food for Peace emergency or refugee food aid, population/UNFPA and Kemp-Kasten funding status, whaling/IWC/Pelly certification, ozone/CFC environmental treaty work, public/congressional/scientific/international-organization context, legal or program authorities, quantities, attached policy papers, and follow-on signed determinations. Keep draft, clearance, signed determination, funding, certification, treaty-negotiation, ratification, and implementation statuses separate; do not flatten these records into generic economic assistance, public-source, treaty, foreign-organization, or country-volume metadata.
29a. Military-crisis and operational records require operational-status treatment. Recent volumes can print policy papers and telegrams that discuss exercises, port calls, deployments, evacuation or strike options, ROE/authorization, host-nation support, military assistance, and named operational programs. Preserve crisis/operation name, theater, units/assets, DOD/JCS/State/NSC lanes, proposal/planning/execution status, cross-volume scheduled-publication references, and document boundaries separately; do not infer that an option was executed, forces deployed, support was delivered, or host-nation approval was obtained without direct evidence.
30. Foreign-government and international-organization records require source-family treatment even when the visible form is a telegram, treaty discussion, translated text, or public/printed item. Recent volumes preserve UN/UNCLOS, OAS, NATO, European Community, IFI, secretariat, foreign ministry, allied-government, delegation, observer, signatory, document-symbol, resolution, communique, draft/adopted, circulation, language/version, and U.S. archival-copy evidence. Keep foreign originals, U.S. reporting wrappers, translated/public versions, conference records, negative-search notes, and adjacent-document boundaries separate; do not infer adoption, official transmission, public release, U.S. receipt, or government endorsement without visible evidence or target-volume authority.
31. Visual-material and source-image evidence requires no-invention treatment. Recent volumes preserve maps, photographs, chart attachments, appendix images, facsimiles, caption/title phrases, source-image targets, printed/not-found/attached-but-not-printed status, visual cross-references, and source-image-only relationships. Keep the visual item's relationship to the selected document separate from ordinary attachment, memcon, table, and appendix metadata; do not invent image content, captions, people, map boundaries, source-image links, appendix targets, or printed status from a textual reference.
32. Release, declassification, source-image, and government-copy evidence requires artifact-specific treatment. Recent volumes can pair RAC scan cautions, NLR/source-control identifiers, declassification stamps, excision statements, source-image or appendix facsimiles, government-copy variants, and `Not found attached` attachment ambiguity with an otherwise normal selected document. Keep release/review metadata, source-image locators, copy variants, sanitization or withdrawal status, not-declassified placeholders, scan quality, and selected-document text separate; do not promote release artifacts or scan identifiers into repository/source-note prose without source authority.
32a. Archival photocopy and source-image uploads require scan-first treatment. Recent volumes can print appendix/source-image facsimiles with sparse OCR, source-control labels, declassification stamps, photocopy artifacts, and separate editor transcriptions. Classify those uploads as `archival_photocopy` when the compiler supplies the scan itself, preserve scan quality and source-image relationship before using OCR, and do not OCR-guess difficult handwriting, faint text, stamps, or marginalia into final annotation prose without supplied transcription or target-volume authority.
32b. Digitized archival/source-record PDFs require counterpart-aware treatment. When a public presidential-library, NARA, CIA Reading Room/CREST, FOIA/MDR, agency-reading-room, or compiler-supplied archival scan appears to be the source record for a document printed in a FRUS volume, classify the PDF as `digitized_archival_record_pdf` and preserve the archival URL, repository, folder title, control identifiers, withdrawal-sheet row, scan quality, and OCR status separately from the history.state.gov printed counterpart. Use FRUS pages as printed-match authority and style evidence, not as accepted PDF fixtures or source-record substitutes. Require title/date/sender-recipient/source-note/folder evidence before marking a match as `matched`; otherwise use `probable_match`, `partial_match`, or `unmatched` and ask for compiler review.
32c. Whole digitized archival folder PDFs require withdrawal-sheet row selection. Recent FRUS source notes can cite a folder while the public archival PDF is the entire folder, with a cover page, withdrawal sheets, multiple rows, and several plausible released or withdrawn items. Classify these uploads as `digitized_archival_folder_pdf`, inventory candidate rows, preserve row ID, description, page count, restriction code, and likely page range, and match the FRUS printed counterpart only when row/heading/date/sender-recipient evidence supports it. Do not treat the folder title or first visible row as the selected document; unresolved row/page ambiguity should become `withdrawal_sheet_row_basis` or `folder_document_inventory_basis` evidence requests.
32d. Related archival folders and alternate copies require divergence handling. Recent source notes can identify a selected source copy while also noting that another copy was sent to a staffer or preserved in a different source family. When a digitized archival PDF shares topic, copied-recipient trail, or public controversy with a printed FRUS document but the uploaded repository, collection, box, folder, visible document text, or source-control path does not match the printed source note, classify it as `related_or_alternate_source_copy_pdf`. Preserve the divergent source path, alternate-copy relationship, and `printed_counterpart_match_status` such as `related_context_only` or `alternate_copy_unverified`; do not mark the PDF as the printed source record or fill the Source note from topical similarity without page-level evidence or compiler authority.
32e. Multi-item folder PDFs need row-to-page mapping. A presidential-library folder PDF can begin with cover pages, withdrawal sheets, folder dividers, and unrelated released items before the selected FRUS source row appears. Build an `archival_folder_row_page_map` that connects withdrawal-sheet row description and page count to visible page spans, headings, sender/recipient, subject, date, and printed-counterpart evidence. Do not draft from the first visible released document after the withdrawal sheet; if the row/body/counterpart link is unclear, request `folder_row_page_map_basis` or compiler page-range authority.
33. Withheld-in-full documents require selected-document treatment without body text. Recent volumes list whole documents withheld after declassification review in chronological place with headings, source notes, and pages-not-declassified counts. Preserve the selected-document identity, source note, classification/handling, review status, page count, and adjacent boundaries; do not classify these items as source-register-only, editorial notes, or OCR failures.
34. Excerpted reports and omitted-body papers require selected-document treatment with explicit scope limits. Recent volumes can print an opening section or summary of an agency paper while stating that the body or remainder is omitted. Preserve the printed excerpt scope, title or control number, source note, classification/handling, portion markings, information-as-of or coordination evidence, omission statement, and adjacent boundaries; do not treat the missing body as OCR failure, a truncated upload, source-register-only evidence, or a withheld-in-full document.
35. Shorthand meeting notes require selected-note treatment. Recent volumes print selected staff or NSC notes with terse fragments, speaker initials, no-classification markings, Daily Diary timing support, original handwritten-note status, and negative-search claims such as `No minutes were found.` Preserve the note transcript and support evidence without expanding initials, polishing fragments, inferring attendees from diary support, or recasting the notes as formal minutes/full memcons.
36. Night notes and evening reports require selected-update treatment. Recent volumes print short updates to the President or other senior officials with undated headings, report/note labels, title or status lines, source-note drafting/clearance or approval dates, and read-status caveats such as `There is no indication Reagan saw this report.` Preserve the intended recipient, date bases, and read-status evidence without treating address to the President as proof of reading, approval, or action.
36a. Mixed or unclear multi-document uploads require selection triage. Recent official PDFs can include the tail of a previous document, several numbered documents of different forms, and the start of a following document in one excerpt. Classify those uploads as `mixed_or_unclear` when the selected range is not supplied, inventory each candidate unit and boundary separately, and ask which document, page range, or packet treatment the compiler selected before drafting final-looking annotation prose.
